# Next games for Giggle Game Night

Research date: 6 October 2026. These are proposed original games, not announced or implemented features. Rankings, scopes and complexity are product recommendations inferred from the sources and the current framework.

The current catalog already has 37 games, including Pocket Karts, Castle Clash, chess, Chain Reaction, Warm Words, Pose Party and Show & Go. Another text-prompt game would add less variety than a relaxed precision game, cooperative action, or a game where the call supplies information needed to win.

## Recommended next three

1. **Cloud Links:** a small, beautiful 3D mini-golf game. It can reuse the new scenery and 3D asset pipeline while giving friends a slower game that leaves room to talk. Start with three excellent holes before expanding courses.
2. **Rocket Room:** an asymmetric cooperative rescue puzzle. Different players see different instructions, so talking through Giggle Meet is the game itself. It needs much less real-time physics than an action game and has strong two-player support.
3. **Parcel Panic:** a cooperative 3D delivery playground. Carrying an awkward object with a friend creates memorable physical comedy. Build a two-player carrying prototype first; multiplayer object ownership and collision quality decide whether to proceed.

Keep improving the existing kart and castle games while prototyping one of these. More catalog entries should follow proven playability, rather than precede it.

## Lessons from reference games

These references establish useful design patterns. Proposed Giggle games would have their own artwork, names, maps, prompts, scoring and implementation.

- **Cooperative levels must scale by actual group size.** PICO PARK 2 officially supports 2–8 players and explicitly tunes level difficulty to the number of participants. Apply that to Switch Trail and Parcel Panic, including a complete two-player route rather than a reduced demo. [PICO PARK 2 — developer site](https://picoparkgame.com/en/pp2/).
- **A level can be part of the social conversation.** Ultimate Chicken Horse combines construction and running and supports custom levels/rules. Scrap Sprint can use the broader build/play pattern while introducing its own cooperative route bonuses and checkpoint rules. [Ultimate Chicken Horse — Clever Endeavour](https://www.cleverendeavourgames.com/ultimate-chicken-horse/).
- **Shared physical tasks create comedy.** Moving Out 2 highlights grabbing, tossing, smashing and accessibility options. Parcel Panic should borrow the lesson of readable cooperative tasks and assist settings, with an original harbor-delivery premise. [Moving Out 2 — publisher site](https://www.team17.com/games/moving-out-2).
- **Compact courses provide variety without a huge world.** Golf With Your Friends is a multiplayer mini-golf reference; Team17 describes themed courses and group play. Cloud Links should begin with a small number of polished holes and a clear aiming interaction. [Golf With Your Friends — Team17](https://www.team17.com/news/team17-is-going-to-egx-2019).
- **Voice becomes essential when information is asymmetric.** Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes gives teammates information the active player cannot see and supports remote play through voice services. Rocket Room should use original puzzle modules and give each role useful private information. [Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes — developer site](https://keeptalkinggame.com/).
- **Communication itself can produce the chaos.** Spaceteam describes its premise as cooperative shouting. Rocket Room can use overlapping responsibilities sparingly after a calm tutorial, without copying its panels or commands. [Spaceteam — developer site](https://spaceteam.ca/).
- **A chase needs rising tension and chances to act.** Among Us Hide n Seek uses tasks, proximity feedback and a final chase phase. Hide & Giggle can use readable proximity cues and required movement to prevent indefinite hiding. [Hide n Seek — Innersloth's official design overview](https://www.innersloth.com/new-game-mode-hide-n-seek-is-here-emergency-meeting-35/amp/).
- **Linked motion is compelling, but network quality matters.** Heave Ho uses gripping and swinging as its core actions. Its original publisher-authored Steam listing describes local/Remote Play support; that does not establish that an independently networked browser version will be easy. Sky Tug needs its own online-physics prototype. [Heave Ho — publisher-authored store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/905340/Heave_Ho/).

## Framework work that these games would justify

Use the existing room, instance/action-window validation, per-player state projection, monotonic server-time display, host controls and disposal contracts. Each game still owns its renderer, art direction and controls. Do not extract an all-purpose game engine before a second actual game proves a shared need.

- **Physics:** keep rendering independent from authoritative simulation. For complex 3D rigid-body contacts, evaluate a pinned Rapier build in a small experiment; it provides 2D/3D WASM physics and does not provide rendering. Its determinism promise requires identical versions, initial state and creation order, and deterministic inputs. It does not remove networking, prediction or correction work. [Rapier getting started](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/getting_started_js/), [Rapier determinism conditions](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/determinism/).
- **2D:** Phaser is a credible renderer/input option. Its Arcade physics is light, but official documentation warns about crowded resting contacts; use Matter or a carefully tested custom controller where stable stacking or constraints are necessary. [Phaser physics overview](https://phaser.io/why-phaser), [Arcade contact limitations](https://docs.phaser.io/api-documentation/class/physics-arcade-arcadephysics).
- **Maps:** use one versioned map definition for collision, spawns, objectives and rendering placement. Give each game real mechanical variations, deterministic seeds where relevant, a validated spawn path and a bounded asset manifest. Map previews should communicate the mechanic: slopes for golf, sightlines for hide-and-seek, chokepoints for deliveries.
- **Calls:** existing Giggle Meet identity/call continues into gameplay. Floating tiles suit golf/chase/action games; spotlight/gallery suits Mirror Relay; role-adjacent tiles suit Rocket Room. A game can request presentation or voice assistance but must not create a second microphone capture. Standalone games remain usable with another call where the core activity permits it.

## Prototype acceptance before adding a catalog card

The first playable must support two people through join → setup → play → results → rematch/change game, and explain the primary action within one screen. Test spectators, late join/reconnect, disconnect while holding an object, duplicate/stale inputs, simultaneous scoring and timeout transitions. Use a bounded authoritative clock and show a completion reason when a goal legitimately ends a round early.

For action games, test representative network delay and packet interruption, actual touch controls, readable small-screen HUD and frame pacing on target devices. Measure results; a smooth run on the Mac mini is not evidence that every phone or Internet connection is smooth. For camera/voice games, include permission refusal, call switching, camera-off viewers and an explicit alternative where feasible.

Finish one attractive map/level and test whether people request a rematch before building ten more. Three visually distinctive, mechanically meaningful maps are preferable to many differently colored copies.

## Deeper prototype directions after the kart battle loop

These deepen the shortlist rather than adding more generic catalog entries. The kart battle research is retained separately in `POCKET_KARTS_BATTLE_RESEARCH_20261006.md`.

### Cloud Links: one course with a memorable rhythm

Make the first three holes a small journey: an open bank-shot garden, a split-route windmill where the safe route costs one stroke, and a downhill funnel with a recovery lane. At two players, alternate shots; other players can aim and watch while one authoritative ball moves. Use a shared rest threshold and a server stroke timeout, so slightly different local velocities cannot decide whose turn it is. A single drag determines direction and power; preview a short aim line, not a complete predicted winning path.

A successful prototype should finish a three-hole match in five to eight minutes, survive reconnect during a rolling ball and make a miss feel recoverable. Begin with fixed geometry and no ball-to-ball collisions; a later simultaneous mode would need a separate fairness test. The floating Giggle Meet tiles can sit beside the shot summary and enlarge during the final score moment. The novelty should come from hole decisions and landscape composition, not many buttons.

### Rocket Room: specific asymmetric information

Give the pilot a visible broken machine with five colored terminals but no legend. The engineer sees the legend and a route constraint but not the terminal arrangement. A second module splits an orbit diagram and a safe launch window; a third asks the team to conserve a shared power budget while deciding which two repairs to prioritize. All symbols, procedures and art are original. With two players, rotate pilot/engineer after each mission; additional players own one meaningful clue each.

Offer a relaxed untimed teaching mission before the timed rescue. The call is core communication; an equivalent text/ping channel enables another-call and accessible play. Keep secret information out of spectators' live views where it would spoil the puzzle. Prove that two first-time players can explain and complete one module without the designer guiding them before authoring a large procedural puzzle pool.

### Parcel Panic: make the carrying problem the first prototype

The first harbor should contain three jobs: a small parcel carried alone, a long parcel requiring two people to coordinate around an L-shaped corner, and a fragile parcel delivered through a gate with a predictable open window. Use one shared loading zone and a visible tide timer. A failed drop resets the parcel to a nearby safe place with a small time cost; it should not silently disappear or permanently fail the mission.

Before building extra districts, test simultaneous grabs, one carrier disconnecting, body/parcel contact in the corner and authoritative delivery credit. If fully physical carrying is unstable online, use bounded attachment/carry rules with cosmetic wobble. Publish that rule clearly rather than showing an apparent rigid object whose collision behaves inconsistently. Voice coordination and reactions are the value; uncontrolled simulation is not required for comedy.

### Pocket Karts Relay: a later objective mode, not another game card

After timed free-for-all combat is stable, test a two-team relay: carry a glowing party lantern between three checkpoints while rivals try to steal it. A carried lantern is visible, slows the kart slightly and prevents offensive use; teammates clear a route. On a knockout it drops at a legal nearby socket, then resets after a bounded idle period. Two-player mode is one kart per team; four to eight players add escorts and interception.

This reuses maps, pickups, respawns and the shared state clock while providing a concrete reason to cross the arena. Validate possession exact-once, drop/reconnect, checkpoint order and timeout ties. It is an original objective variation inspired by the broad range of battle objectives Nintendo documents, not a new engine project. [Mario Kart 8 Deluxe battle overview](https://www.nintendo.com/au/games/nintendo-switch/mario-kart-8-deluxe/).
