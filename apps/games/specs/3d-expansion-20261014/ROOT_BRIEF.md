## A. Cloud Links — build first,2–8 players

Social mini-golf: everyone putts at once on the same course. Balls do not physically collide in the first version. Everyone shares a hole start and deadline, can see friends' shots, and gets a short hole recap. Lowest strokes across the course wins; equal scores share rank. Balls must be distinguished by patterned rings or markings as well as colour. Explain the non-colliding rule once in onboarding.

Supersede the Oct6 sequential-shot proposal explicitly. Simultaneous play reduces idle time but needs readable friends' balls, fair moving hazards, crowd-free aim and early-finisher spectating. First polished slice is one bank-shot hole with a safe two-shot route and a possible one-shot risky bank. One ramp/contoured surface must produce actual3D movement and collision, not a flat picture. The three-hole release can later add a shared-phase windmill and downhill funnel; author those only after the slice is enjoyable.

Aim and power with one drag gesture, cancel safely, optional keyboard aim/power/confirm. During aiming the camera must remain stable; separate camera pan/follow/reset from shot input. Follow the local interpolated ball after release; holed players can follow a friend without affecting play. No ball-to-ball collisions, kart power-ups, inventory grind or course editor in this slice. Server owns shots, rest, cup entry, out-of-bounds/reset, penalties, stroke cap and deadline. A rolling ball cannot accept a second shot. Propose precise rest thresholds and high-speed cup-contact tests rather than deciding rest in the renderer.

Visual direction: clean sunny toy-like course, saturated emerald turf, turquoise edges, warm yellow flags and coral accents, consistent bevels and soft contact shadows. Landscaped3D surroundings with clear ramps and paths; readable matte ball silhouette rather than a glowing dot. Source actual course pieces from the licensed Kenney Minigolf Kit; inspect selected assets and tune a consistent palette. This is a practical art direction, not a photoreal cinematic screenshot target. HUD: hole/par/strokes at an edge, one small deadline, player standings collapsed; power indicator near the ball only while aiming. The playfield stays clear.

## B. Rocket Room — second,2–6 players

Cooperative spaceship repair. Different players have complementary information and controls; clear communication through Giggle Meet is a core mechanic. Equivalent text and contextual pings must complete the same mission. Do not start another microphone/camera capture or record a call.

First slice: one original repair module, untimed teaching attempt then a timed rescue. Pilot sees3D terminals/layout, engineer sees connection constraints; neither can solve alone by reading the same screen. At2players both interpret and act; at3–6 players assign meaningful control/decision ownership rather than extra spectators holding scraps of text. Specify a roster-to-responsibility table and demonstrate the6player version before content expansion. Successful repairs visibly restore power/ship components. Include forgiving wrong-action feedback, bounded hints, explicit loss/retry and role rotation. Reassign a disconnected clue owner's responsibilities predictably, keeping private information out of public/spectator/report projections.

Use a fixed inspectable3D ship/repair station, not free-roaming FPS movement. Original puzzle symbols with shape/text alternatives to colour. Visual direction: saturated midnight-blue ship, coral panels, amber power and mint success, legible light labels, compact tangible instruments. Use licensed space props for the actual3D scene; precise repair controls should be interactive components rather than an image of a dashboard. HUD shows only mission progress and remaining time; the role's active instrument and a collapsible communication panel sit at the edge. Keyboard-open phone layouts must retain the current clue/control and send action. Voice-panel placement may differ from golf because the call is central.

## C. Bomb Garden — third,2–8 players

Short3D arena rounds: move, plant timed bombs, clear cover and escape predictable blast chains. Overhead camera exposes dangerous routes; explosions must not be hidden by scenery. Start with one readable arena and one bomb type, no progression/pickup catalogue. Use authoritative grid occupancy/explosion logic with smoothly animated3D characters; evaluate whether movement itself is fun before elaborate art. Do not force Rapier onto inherently discrete bomb rules.

Target45second rounds, best of3, survivor wins; simultaneous final deaths draw; deadline policy must be deterministic and visible. Specify exact planted-bomb re-entry, fuse, wall/cover blocking, chain ordering, simultaneous deaths and disconnect occupancy rules. Safe spawns must include enough escape space before the first bomb; do not allow a random seed to create unavoidable death. Results wait for the last gameplay explosion to settle visibly. Dead players can follow friends and ready for the next round without changing survival outcomes. Measure actual eliminated-player waiting; if it frequently exceeds30seconds or humans stop rematching, revise the mode before release. Do not quietly add a fairness-changing ghost mechanic.

Local input acknowledgement must be distinct from authoritative bomb confirmation/rejection. Do not render an apparently successful escape that the server repeatedly reverses into death. Movement/bomb controls must work through keyboard and touch, including blocked UI/modal input, multi-touch, cancellation and blur. Visual direction: bright landscaped arena, cobalt walls, lime ground, tangerine bomb glow/fuse, animated licensed mascot characters with readable outlines. Bomb has a clear physical silhouette and wick; use proper models/authored meshes, not a tiny generic orb. Avoid screen-filling bloom/radius drawings and a boxed HTML board. Minimal HUD: lives/round score, compact fuse/danger cues in the world, one timer, contextual one-time controls hint.

## Common architecture and quality policy

Existing repository uses Three.js0.186.1, Rapier0.21.0, Node24 and authoritative Railway WebSocket rooms with Vercel static assets. Reuse established room identity, instance/window guards, server clock, lifecycle/disposal, input-capability handling, report/kick/end controls and Giggle Meet mediation. Each game can have its own visual language. No React/engine migration or speculative universal game engine. Shared10Hz snapshots do not mean meaningful physics should run at10Hz: propose a fixed simulation step, bounded catch-up and an explicit render/interpolation boundary. Server owns outcomes; camera and effects never judge scoring. Use Rapier for golf contacts where justified, discrete rules for bombs, puzzle authority for repairs. Do not assume determinism eliminates prediction/reconciliation work.

Quality stages: (G0) reviewed spec/reference/asset plan; (G1) two people enjoy the central mechanic in one greybox; (G2) one polished level with real assets/camera/HUD/audio; (G3) independent behaviour, native visual/input, physical-device, network and human-fun acceptance; (G4) exact-source release candidate and verified matching deployed bytes. A catalogue entry requiresG4. Advance only with evidence; explicitly list unverified checks. No mass parallel game implementation. Planning/assets/QA can run in parallel; prove one new runtime slice before multiplying systems.

Every game must cover normal join/setup/play/natural results/rematch/change/end/leave; late watcher/reconnect/host migration/kick; stale/duplicate input and old timers; focus/blur/modal; hidden/offscreen readiness; pending/rejected sends; equal scores/zero-score results; viewport/orientation/safe-area/keyboard; media panel; context loss and asset failure. Countdown starts after required assets, physics and actual first rendered frame; preload/warm effects so first shot/explosion does not freeze the frame. Readiness expiry must recover clearly rather than overlap countdown text.

Desktop, portrait phone, landscape phone, tablet and compact embedded layouts are distinct review states. Width controls layout, not device/input identity. Touch-capable laptops retain keyboard/mouse; touch controls follow actual capability/preference. Lock page movement only inside active play interactions; drawers/menus still scroll normally. No homepage/navigation click sounds. Gameplay audio is event-driven, audible but comfortable, stops on disposal, and ducks around conversation. Reduced-motion alternatives preserve game information.

Initial measurable targets (proposed, not observed): critical compressed first-play payload≤4 MB including required engine/WASM/fonts/audio; usable cold playfield≤5 seconds on a declared10Mbps/100 ms profile with visible loading throughout. Desktop60FPS target with p95 frame interval≤20 ms; physical midrange phone30FPS target with p95≤36 ms, plus10minute thermal play. Local feedback≤50 ms, separate server acknowledgement timing. Bound physics CPU/network/entity/effect allocations and measure maximum roster plus declared concurrent-room load; do not assign a hard draw-call count without the scene/device measurement. Test nominal,150/300 ms RTT with jitter, interrupted snapshot delivery and3second disconnect. WebSocket runs over ordered TCP; do not pretend arbitrary application-packet reordering is the normal transport.

Asset gates: source URL/license per actual file, selected pack coherence, GLB units/pivots/materials, explicit collision proxies, critical vs optional asset manifests, dimensions/bytes/decode cost, mobile texture/geometry/LOD limits, no runtime primitive model-building work during attacks. Generated images may support cards, icons or textures when appropriate; no full gameplay image pasted behind fake controls.

Human gate: first-time people can start and perform the main action without designer coaching; record what they misunderstand, involuntary waiting, unfair outcomes and whether they voluntarily rematch. Root must assess actual playfeel/audio/animation/HUD from native captures. Unit tests prove rules, screenshots prove selected visuals, resized desktop proves layout, and physical touch/heat/voice require corresponding real evidence. All newQA cases default PLANNED with no claimed run.
