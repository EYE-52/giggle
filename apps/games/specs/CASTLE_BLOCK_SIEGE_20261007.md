# Castle Clash — block siege design and acceptance

This replaces the two-team plot/tower game. The owner explicitly requested 2–4 individual castles, free stacking of differently sized blocks, and block/weapon cards. Codex owns design and acceptance; Muse may implement precisely bounded tasks. This is a complete browser game, with real room lifecycle and mobile controls, not a visual demo.

## Match and victory

Each player owns a castle and a Crown crystal. Two castles face each other, three form a triangle, four form a square. No arbitrary four-player minimum or teammates in this mode. Up to four active seats, later joiners watch and may join the next match.

Three rounds, each with a construction phase followed by a siege. Build starts with 60 seconds and can end early only when all connected, living players explicitly press Ready. Siege lasts up to 90 seconds. Destroying a Crown knocks that player out for the round. The last Crown standing wins immediately, after the final impact/collapse animation. Otherwise, the healthiest Crown wins the round; ties use stable standing height, then damage dealt, then a declared draw. Height is a useful risk/reward choice, never an incentive to stack an unsupported thin pole and ignore defense. The match winner has most round wins; remaining ties use cumulative Crown health and damage, then a shared win.

Construction feels creative: place blocks on the island, rotate them, stack them on valid top surfaces, remove a recent block or undo the last placement. A safe default foundation surrounds the Crown but does not prebuild the player's castle. A small first-match guide illustrates three actions: choose a block, tap to place/stack, choose a weapon and aim at a rival. It disappears once dismissed. Help remains available.

## Blocks, cards, and structural rules

Use physical-looking rectangular stone, timber and iron blocks, not predefined tower upgrades. The starting hand contains five shapes: cube1×1×1, beam2×1×1, slab2×2×0.5, pillar1×1×2, parapet2×1×1. All dimensions are world units. Card thumbnails show the actual shape, material color and a short quantity. A hand contains finite quantities rather than money, experience, unlock forms or text-heavy dashboards. Provide enough blocks for a meaningful castle (at least 24 placements per person) and a bounded maximum64 pieces per castle. Server owns hand inventory.

Placement snaps to half-unit coordinates within a12×12 island build zone, up to12 units high. A ghost and compact Rotate control show exact proposed placement. A legal placement needs ground or enough supporting top-face overlap, no overlap with an existing piece or Crown, no outside-zone footprint. Unsupported midair placements are rejected clearly. Rotate is90 degrees around the up axis. Desktop mouse and touch tap both work; drag/orbit/pinch never places a block. Keyboard1–5 selects cards, R rotates, Escape cancels ghost; these remain usable at any resolution. Explicit camera drag threshold separates a tap from orbit.

Authoritative support graph is resolved after structural damage and removal. A destroyed support can bring dependent blocks down: find supported pieces from the ground upward, then collapse unsupported pieces exactly once and remove them from collision/standing-height accounting. Client animates the authoritative falling/debris events; it does not decide support or scoring. Do not claim rigid-body physics if the shipped rules are deterministic structural support. This bounded structural approach is the chosen first production implementation; a general WASM physics engine should be added only if needed for real behavior.

Materials: timber35HP, stone65HP, iron95HP; iron cards are scarce. Support overlap must cover at least30% of the placed footprint and include its center of mass (overlap union, not counting the same area twice). Placement/undo/removal only changes the actor's own hand and castle. On removal/collapse, unsupported pieces do not refund inventory during siege. Two rapid actions cannot spend the same last card twice.

## Siege interaction

The hand becomes three distinct weapon cards: ballista bolt, mortar shell and rocket. All start usable; exact ammunition and cooldown are clear in the card, with a progress ring. Suggested starting ammunition per round: ballista8, mortar4, rockets2; ballista refills one every5seconds up to8 so players never wait with nothing to do. Aiming is a deliberate enemy point selected in the world, with a trajectory/reticle preview, then Fire. No endless instruction banner. Pick a target castle via its visible flag or compact opponent badge; chosen target/camera persist across snapshots.

Ballista fires a fast slender physical bolt (single-block pressure,34 damage). Mortar is an arcing iron canister (20 direct/12 bounded nearby damage, good against supported clusters). Rocket has fins, metallic body and a short exhaust trail (52 direct/18 nearby damage), fewer shots. Fire originates at a readable actual siege launcher beside the actor's Crown. Server computes trajectory and the FIRST solid block/Core collision; selecting a Crown does not bypass defenses. Bounded segment sweeps prevent tunneling. Friendly fire is disabled. Shielding/occlusion of splash is explicit and tested. Every accepted shot has an ID, deterministic start/flight/impact timestamp and one impact/collapse event sequence. Server resolves delayed impacts before declaring the result; players see the completed effects before victory UI/confetti.

No teleporting cubes, unexplained UI switches, instant invisible destruction, plain glowing orb rockets or drawn explosion-radius circles. Impacts have a small directional burst, stone/wood debris, readable Crown damage, short shake bounded by reduced-motion preference, and distinct audio. Eliminated castles remain as damaged scenery; watcher controls remain available. The clock is authoritative; a result includes the legitimate reason (last Crown standing / health decision / draw), with no obsolete time-left display.

## Exact visual direction

Full-screen original stylized 3D alpine arena: lavender mountains and slow cloud silhouettes, visible sunset sun, saturated green islands and turquoise water. Castles are at the polygon corners around an open center. Decorative terrain never obscures target castles. Flags and Crown crystals use coral, mint, yellow and violet with distinct glyphs for color-independent identification. Actual stone/timber grain, bevels, battlements/flag accents, metal highlights and dimensional weapon silhouettes. Avoid the old beige color, plot grids, standard website cards and paragraphs. Three.js is already installed and appropriate; changing to Godot is optional only with a justified browser/network/performance benefit.

HUD stays mounted: compact phase/round/clock at top center, own Crown status top left below the platform Room launcher, compact rivals top right, five block cards or three weapon cards bottom center. Cards have rendered original3D thumbnails or properly prepared original raster assets; no raw emoji substitute for weapon art. Selected cards raise slightly and highlight, disabled cards still show a clear quantity or cooldown. Cards and actual controls≥44px. Desktop uses five cards side by side. Phone portrait uses a shallow horizontal card hand with thumb-reachable Rotate/Place or Fire. Short landscape keeps the world visible, with no overlapping controls. Canvas is full bleed; HTML overlays may be used for semantic controls but their presentation must feel like a game hand and HUD.

Camera overview must show every castle. Own/target camera shortcuts, bounded orbit, pinch zoom, keyboard access. Snapshots and half-second clock ticks never reset selection, camera, target, hand focus or draft placement. Async asset load may enhance existing meshes; it must never replace the entire UI with an old2D game. WebGL failure keeps the same HUD around one explicit readable fallback, without bouncing or replaying actions.

## Framework and ownership

Implement a reusable configured `createBlockSiege` authority module under games/engines and a matching scene/controller. Castle's server file should be a thin manifest/configuration adapter. Use the existing Room, seat limits, instance/window nonces, monotonic server clock, asset loader, arena-controls, results presentation and disposal contracts. Do not build another socket, room, media capture or generic mega-engine. Keep existing `createBuildBattle` available for its existing consumers; this new block game has a different rule model and must not silently change their API.

Do not edit Karts, Chain, audio, app.js/shared CSS, package files or deploy. Report any genuinely necessary shared change to ROOT. Preserve old Castle source copies; replace tests tied to the old DOM only with stronger tests of the new real behavior. Keep useful generic Room/SDK tests intact.

## Acceptance before release

Run real2-,3-,4-player matches: join→setup→build→ready/clock→aim/fire→damage/collapse→round result→new round→final result→rematch/change game. Include late watcher, reconnect, disconnect, simultaneous last-card spending, simultaneous lethal impacts, replay/stale-window refusal, double Fire, out-of-bounds/overlap/support rejection, inventory consistency and deterministic support/first-hit/splash rules. A player may not be scored twice or control another castle. Actor input is neutralized on menu/visibility/fullscreen/dispose.

Root reviews actual browser pixels and input at320×640,390×844,568×320,844×390 and desktop; mouse-only small laptop, touch laptop with keyboard default and phone touch controls. Full-screen and leave/change actions must settle clearly. Record frame pacing/render counters and bound mesh/projectile/debris pools; no unsupported 'lag-free', 'bug-free', physical-iPhone or competitor-parity claim. Complete one attractive arena and a fun full match before adding other arenas. Use the parent's native CUA browser for actual acceptance, never shell Playwright/Puppeteer.

Research informing the design: construction, weak-point attacks and visible rubble are central to [Forts' official gameplay](https://www.earthworkgames.com/). Original models may use the [Kenney Castle Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/castle-kit), whose official page identifies the pack as CC0; retain source/license/hash records and ship only needed optimized assets. These references are design evidence, not permission to copy another game's brand, maps or assets.
